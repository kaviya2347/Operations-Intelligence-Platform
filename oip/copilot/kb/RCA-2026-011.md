# Summary
From 20 to 24 April 2026, average handle time on Meridian Cards rose by about a third, from roughly 290 seconds to 400 seconds. Staffing was planned for normal handle time, so service level fell to below 50% on the worst days and abandon rate rose above 20%. The cause was response-time degradation in the client's card management system after an infrastructure change.

# Findings
- Handle-time inflation affected every agent and every interval, which pointed to a system cause rather than a people cause.
- Volume was in line with forecast and staffed agents met the requirement, so neither volume nor staffing explained the breaches.
- Screen response times logged by agents were 6 to 9 seconds per lookup against a normal 1 to 2 seconds.

# Actions taken during the incident
1. P1 ticket raised with the client's IT service desk on day one (SOP-OPS-03).
2. Callback offer activated for all waiting callers above 90 seconds (SOP-OPS-04).
3. Cross-skilled agents pulled back from KYC back office to voice in peak intervals.
4. Non-urgent requests (reward points, limit enquiries) moved to callback to free agents for card blocks and disputes.

# Prevention
1. Client to share change calendars for card systems with the delivery centre.
2. Handle-time anomaly alert at queue level, so latency shows within a day.
3. A latency playbook: when AHT rises more than 20% across all agents, re-run the interval requirement with the new AHT and request overtime immediately instead of waiting for the weekly review.

# Lesson
When handle time rises for everyone at once, look at systems first. Coaching cannot fix a slow screen.
